# Non-Linear Functions vs. Matrices

> [!NOTE] This page is intended for non-mathematical audiences.
A more detailed explanation can be found [here](./what-is-a-fractal-flame.md#nlf-vs-mat), and an overview of FractalFlame (including these concepts) can be found [here](./what-is-fractalflame.md).

In FractalFlame, there are two kinds of mathematical objects with the most largest impact on the shape of the fractal: Non-Linear Functions (NLFs) and Matrices. The details of their individual effects are near-impossible to categorize and are best learned through [experience](https://xentempest.github.io/FractalFlame/FractalFlame.html). However, there are some important differences to consider:

### Matrices
Matrices are randomly generated objects and cannot be chosen specifically. It can take more randomization than one may expect to achieve desirable results.

> [!INFO] Matrices have the largest effect on the fractal, but very different effects from each other. When you find an appealing pairing of Matrices and NLFs, keep exploring! Chances are there are other wildly different options waiting for you.

### Non-Linear Functions
NLFs, by contrast, are unique, constant objects, created by individual people (mostly [Scott Draves](https://flam3.com/flame_draves.pdf)). Their effects are "the same" on all fractals, though depending on which other NLFs and Matrices they are interacting with, those effects may appear distinct. Some NLFs (like "Waves", "Julia", and others) have associated sliders, which allow their behavior to be modified, sometimes considerably.

> [!INFO] Remember to try different parameter values. Though some NLFs, like "Waves," don't change much with different values, others, like "Julia," are almost unrecognizable at different values. It is worth trying many variations of a successful set of matrices and NLFs.
